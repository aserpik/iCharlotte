The objections previously raised regarding Request for Production Nos. 1 through 5 and 7 apply with equal force to Request No. 6, yet this particular request introduces even greater concerns regarding overbreadth. By seeking every document related to Pinscreen’s valuation over a nearly one-year period, the Plaintiff has cast an impermissibly wide net. Crucially, the Plaintiff has failed to offer any substantive justification for demanding sensitive financial data that spans the entirety of his employment through his termination. Not only was the Plaintiff never entitled to such proprietary information as a function of his role at the company, but the requested data is also fundamentally irrelevant to his claims of fraudulent inducement. Because these financial records post-date the Plaintiff’s acceptance of employment, they cannot possibly speak to the representations made to him at the time of his hiring. Consequently, this request is nothing more than an unjustified intrusion into the company’s private financial history.
