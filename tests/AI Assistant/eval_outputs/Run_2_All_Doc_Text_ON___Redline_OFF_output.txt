The arguments precluding discovery for Requests 1 through 5 and 7 apply with even greater force to Request No. 6, which represents a significant and unjustified expansion in scope. While the previous requests were problematic, Request No. 6 is exponentially broader, demanding every document related to Pinscreen’s valuation over a nearly one-year period. Plaintiff has offered no justification for such a sweeping intrusion into the company’s private financial records. During his tenure, Plaintiff was never the Chief Financial Officer, nor did he hold any position that entitled him to access sensitive financial data as part of his job duties. Most importantly, the requested information is legally irrelevant to the core of this dispute. Plaintiff’s claims center on fraudulent inducement—the allegation that he was misled into accepting employment. By definition, any financial data generated after he had already accepted his position and commenced work could not have induced him to join the company. This request is therefore nothing more than an overreaching attempt to gain access to confidential information that has no bearing on the legal claims at issue.
