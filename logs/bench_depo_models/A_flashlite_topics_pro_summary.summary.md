The parties took the deposition of Pamela Forney on September 24, 2025. Below are the most salient portions of Pamela Forney's testimony:

**Mechanism of Subject Foot Injury**
- Treating Physician woke up at approximately 3:00 a.m. on January 4, 2022, to use the restroom. Treating Physician wore compression socks without shoes while walking from the bedroom.
- Treating Physician stepped on a carpet nail or tack with the right foot. The nail was located on the transition strip between the bedroom carpet and the bathroom floor.
- The carpet was pulled away from the stripping but covered the nail prior to the incident. Treating Physician did not see the nail before stepping on it.
- The nail punctured the ball of the right foot beneath the toes. The injury caused immediate, severe pain and bleeding.
- Treating Physician sat on the toilet to clean the wound with alcohol. The bleeding stopped after approximately 30 minutes, and Treating Physician applied a circular bandage before returning to bed.

**Pre-Existing Spine Condition and Employment**
- Treating Physician suffered a lower spine injury at seven years old after being stepped on. This childhood injury never required surgical intervention.
- The spine condition causes ongoing pain and prevented Treating Physician from continuing employment. Treating Physician began receiving Social Security disability benefits in 2003 as a result of this condition.
- Treating Physician last worked in 2002 for an investment company called Melons in Sacramento. Prior employment included working as a re-entry specialist for foster children and a correctional officer at Terminal Island Prison.
- Treating Physician previously received pain management treatment for issues involving the left pelvic bone and the knee. This pre-existing treatment included prescriptions for Norco at a dosage of five to seven milligrams.
- Treating Physician did not use a cane, walker, or any other mobility assistance devices prior to the incident. Treating Physician maintained independent mobility despite the lower back condition.

**Management Knowledge of Carpet Defects**

- Treating Physician showed the bedroom carpet nails to a maintenance supervisor named Jessie Valdez. Management employee Ruby accompanied the Carpet Express supervisor during the second visit but did not order the removal or replacement of the affected carpet.

**Medical Treatment and Specialist Referrals**
- Treating Physician sought initial emergency treatment for right foot pain at Foothill Presbyterian Hospital on January 5, 2022. The hospital staff provided antibiotics, wrapped the foot, and performed x-rays that showed no retained foreign objects.
- Treating Physician presented to the Barstow Community Hospital emergency department in a wheelchair on January 14, 2022. Doctors at this facility prescribed a course of antibiotics to address the ongoing foot swelling and pain.
- Treating Physician returned to Barstow Community Hospital on January 27, 2022, complaining of burning, throbbing, and stinging pain. An examining doctor suggested the pain could be related to diabetic neuropathy.
- Primary care physician Dr. Dawit Mamo referred Treating Physician to neurologist Dr. Gallegos and foot/ankle specialist Dr. Dana Day. Dr. Day eventually discharged Treating Physician after concluding no further podiatric interventions would improve the condition.
- Treating Physician underwent testing at a diabetic care facility in Apple Valley specifically to evaluate for lower extremity neuropathy. The Apple Valley facility determined Treating Physician did not have diabetic neuropathy in the legs.

**History of Diabetes and Medication**
- Treating Physician received a Type 2 diabetes diagnosis in 2005. Initial treatment consisted of daily oral Metformin without insulin dependence.
- Doctors transitioned Treating Physician from Metformin to insulin injections in late 2006. Treating Physician developed insulin resistance approximately one year later, causing dangerously high A1C levels.
- Medical providers increased the short-acting insulin dosage from 10 to 16 units to counteract the insulin resistance. Treating Physician maintained this 16-unit dosage through the date of the incident and monitored blood sugar levels twice daily.
- Primary care physician Dr. Mamo warned Treating Physician about the severe risks associated with uncontrolled diabetes. These warnings highlighted potential damage to vision and multiple organ systems.
- Treating Physician's doctors noted concerns about an elevated A1C level of 8.9 or higher during post-incident evaluations. Treating Physician acknowledged that a safe A1C level falls between six and seven.

**Post-Incident Functional Limitations**
